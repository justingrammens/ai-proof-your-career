## What the talk covers

I see this shift from two seats: hiring software engineers at Lab651, and teaching new professionals at St. Thomas. From both, the people thriving with AI aren't the most technical. They moved first.

1. **The entry-level job just changed.** AI now does first drafts, research and summaries. Evaluating AI's work is the new junior job.
2. **The data is in.** Employment is down for 22 to 25 year olds in the most AI-exposed jobs, driven by reduced hiring rather than layoffs.
3. **The market is splitting into two tracks.** Demand and wage premiums for AI skills are rising fast, and AI-capable companies are hiring more.
4. **It's early innings.** What AI could do far outpaces what it's doing today, so the window to get ahead is still open.
5. **Here's your opening.** Most leaders would now pick a less experienced candidate with AI skills over a more experienced one without them.
6. **What I actually ask in interviews now.** Four questions that reveal whether AI is really part of how you work.
7. **What AI can't replace.** Judgment, accountability, relationships, taste and domain depth, plus where experience comes from now.
8. **The human in the loop is the deliverable.** A story from an AI claims system we built, and product-minded work in any field.
9. **What to do this month.** One assistant, four levels, one level a week for 30 days, five moves that compound, and starting something of your own.

The closing line: **the advantage is you.**
